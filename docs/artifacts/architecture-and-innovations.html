The finding that shapes everything

Two public systems already solve the naive version of this problem. One of them solves it very well.

Flat semantic retrieval over a standards corpus is commoditized. It is table stakes, not a differentiator. Standards360 has to win above the retrieval layer.

BIS-COMPASS was scored against an organiser rubric of Hit@3 > 80%, MRR@5 > 0.7, latency < 5 s. Assume a similar rubric here. The strategy follows directly: adopt that proven stack verbatim on day one, clear the rubric cheaply, and spend the remaining nine days on everything the rubric does not measure.

§3

Five innovations, ranked by build order

Each had to clear all five tests: visible in an eight-minute demo, technically defensible, solves real DoCA pain, buildable in ten days, and unlikely to appear in any competing submission.

I1

Specification Gap Auditor

The system does not primarily answer “which standard applies?” It answers “what is wrong with this tender?”

Read the problem statement literally: specifications “omit relevant standards, reference outdated versions, or include incomplete technical requirements.” Those are three audit findings, not three search results. Every competing team will demo a search box. We demo a reviewer — using the judge's own uploaded document. This framing shift is the highest-leverage decision in the project.

Build cost
Medium — rides on the clause segmenter we need anyway
Demo moment
Judge uploads a live GeM tender → six concrete findings in 20 seconds
I2

Temporal Compliance Engine

Every recommendation is snapped to the current version with amendments applied — and QCO obligation is evaluated against the tender's own delivery timeline, not today's date.

Tender cites IS XXXX:2018. Current is IS XXXX:2025 + Amendment 1.
Delivery date 15-Sep-2026 falls after the QCO enforcement date of 01-Jul-2026 —
BIS certification is mandatory for this award.

This is precisely what a vector database structurally cannot do, and precisely what causes real procurement disputes. The underlying data is authoritative — BIS lifecycle records and QCO notifications — so it is schema and rules rather than ML, which makes it cheap, verifiable, and impossible to hallucinate.

Build cost
Low to medium — authoritative data, deterministic logic
Demo moment
The version-drift panel catching a genuinely outdated citation
I3

Citation-Closure Knowledge Graph

Allied standards are computed as a typed k-hop closure over a graph derived from Clause 2 of every standard, with each edge traceable to the clause that asserts it.

“Identify allied standards” is a literal requirement of the problem statement, and it is provably not an embedding problem — a transitive closure is not a nearest-neighbour query. Per-clause attribution simultaneously produces the audit trail that CVC and CAG would demand. Grounded in published work: RefWalk citation-closure retrieval (EMNLP 2026 Findings) and multi-agent knowledge-graph construction for regulatory QA.

Build cost
High — this is the real engineering; extraction F1 must be measured, not assumed
Demo moment
The expanding subgraph, each edge labelled with its source clause
I4

Restrictive-Specification Detector

Flag tenders whose parameter combination is satisfiable by implausibly few products — the signature of a specification written around a single vendor.

No competing team will have this. It speaks to the Ministry and to the Central Vigilance Commission, not only to the procurement officer, which converts the tool from a productivity aid into a governance instrument — and that is what carries a Ministry-sponsored problem statement into the finals. The technical story stays clean: compare the ranges a tender demands against the ranges the applicable standard actually permits, and flag over-constraint.

Build cost
Medium — a rule-based v1 grounded in permitted value ranges is enough
Demo moment
“3 of 9 parameters are narrower than IS permits — this combination matches an unusually small product space”
I5

Standards Impact Radar

Invert the graph. Given a standard about to be revised, show every downstream standard and every live tender that cites it.

This one serves BIS itself rather than the procurement officer. Showing the sponsor a view of their own workflow is the strongest available signal of product understanding — and because it reuses the graph already built for I3, the marginal cost is close to zero.

Build cost
Low — the graph already exists, traversed in reverse
Demo moment
“Revising IS 456 affects 34 downstream standards and 12 live tenders”
§4

Deliberately not innovations

Naming these protects the timebox and keeps the pitch honest.

  • Multilingual inputA stated requirement, and bge-m3 provides it essentially for free. Ship it; do not pitch it as novel.
  • Hybrid retrieval + rerankingTable stakes with public prior art. Adopt it, clear the rubric, move on.
  • A chatbot interfaceThe main screen is an analyzer. A chat window signals a demo; a findings report signals a system.
