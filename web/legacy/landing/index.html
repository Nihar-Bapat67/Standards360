What goes wrong today

A tender is only as good as the standards it names.

  • Standards are cited years after they were withdrawn.
  • The product standard is named and the test methods that prove conformity are left out.
  • Certification that is compulsory by law goes unmentioned.

One line from a tender

The same requirement, resolved.

As written

Supply of 500 MT of 43 grade ordinary Portland cement for RCC work, as per IS 8112:1989

Standard

IS 269:2015 — Ordinary portland cement (Sixth Revision) IS 8112 was withdrawn and its subject merged into IS 269:2015, which covers the 33, 43 and 53 grades together.

Test methods

IS 4031, IS 4032 Physical tests and chemical analysis for hydraulic cement. Both are named as references inside IS 269 itself, and a tender that cites only the product standard leaves the conformity untestable.

Sampling

IS 3535 Methods of sampling hydraulic cement.

Certification

Compulsory BIS certification Quality Control Order notified and in force since 17 February 2003. 48 recognised laboratories can carry out the testing. Bidders must hold a valid licence, and this belongs in the tender as an eligibility condition.

Every figure above is read from the BIS catalogue record for IS 269:2015.

How it works

Four steps, and no guesswork in any of them.

01

Understand the tender

Reads a PDF, a pasted specification, a screenshot or a bare product name, and extracts what was specified along with what was left unsaid.

02

Search meaning, not keywords

Matches the description against the clauses of the standards themselves, so a requirement phrased in a tender's own words still finds the right specification.

03

Check currency and certification

Resolves every standard against the BIS catalogue: the edition in force, its amendments, the replacement chain, and the Quality Control Order that applies.

04

Produce the annexure

Returns the officer's own document with an Annexure — Applicable Indian Standards appended, carrying the evidence, the warnings and the confidence band.

What makes it different

It reasons about the officer's work, not just the catalogue.

It grades the standards already cited

Each citation the tender carries is judged on three questions — does it exist, is it current, is it relevant to this product — and returned as keep, replace, remove or add, with the reason attached.

Allied standards arrive by role

Test method, terminology, normative reference, installation, related product. Grouped by the part each plays, rather than flattened into one undifferentiated list.

It states its confidence, and asks

Where the description is too thin to answer well, it says so and puts two short questions to the officer instead of returning a weak answer with a confident face.

Every number is checked before it is printed

Recommended standards come from database rows, never from a model. Generated prose is filtered against the whole catalogue, and any number that cannot be verified is removed rather than softened.
